# Expectation: course evaluation and source audit

## Search and selection boundary

Six located university candidates were compared for this chapter. Four written courses were selected as the core synthesis: Oxford, MIT, UC Berkeley, and Carnegie Mellon. Cornell supplies an additional complete finite-experiment lecture check. Stanford's located Moments page was screened, but the locally acquired PDF was a different random-variable lecture and is excluded from the expectation-source count. This is a documented accessible pool, not a claim to have reviewed every university course in existence.

Candidates were ranked for this chapter by exact hypotheses, depth of proofs, relevance to mathematical/conceptual examination families, clarity of definitions and boundaries, and actually accessible written content. Existing cached PDFs were reread in the new expectation scopes; a previous chapter's reading did not automatically qualify them for this chapter. A syllabus, index, web snippet, or unverified slide title is insufficient evidence.

## Source corrections and reconciliations

Introductory sources sometimes call an infinite mean nonexistent. The chapter explicitly distinguishes finite integrable expectation, a defined extended mean, and undefined signed expectation. Absolute convergence is required for ordinary finite signed algebra; nonnegative sums permit extended values.

MIT uses failures-before-success geometric counts, whereas positive-trial counts begin at one. The chapter states the offset and boundary at success probability zero. Finite caps, success conditioning, and failure return codes are separate measurements.

Oxford page 28 gives the exact loop sum but informally calls it close to log n. Its exact expression is $H_{2n}-H_n/2$, with leading coefficient one half on $\ln n$. The independent solution retains that coefficient. CMU page 8 prints the standard-deviation symbol instead of its square in the perfectly dependent sample-average variance example. Since the average then equals the common variable, its variance is the original variance, not its standard deviation.

Cornell's expectation function on all real-valued maps is used within its finite-experiment setting. A countable or general-space extension is restricted to integrable maps for a finite real result. Product factorization likewise carries independence and integrability conditions. The lesson does not infer independence from zero covariance or one matching product moment.

## Examination evidence and exercise coverage

Two authentic English adaptations were visually checked against their original PDF pages from repository commit `bdadf6e2c9cadc4772ae137a96a3da753c7cfd08`: PhD Computer Science 1404 Q72, page 16, running-minimum update expectation; and MSc Computer Science 1405 Q121, page 26, a weighted binomial sum that also admits a factorial-moment proof. Original option order is retained. Answers are independently derived, not official keys. The second question's auxiliary binomial experiment is clearly labelled as a solving device rather than an original premise.

The 80 independently written course-family or original problems cover weighted laws, integrability, nonlinear payoffs, common-outcome linearity, dependent products, indicators, permutations, records, occupancy, sampling, runs, graphs, tails, waiting contracts, moments, prediction loss, bounds, mixtures, random lengths, infinite sums, size bias and numerical stability. They are not a reproduction of every copyrighted course exercise. Foundational calibration and medium-to-hard synthesis problems are distinguished from any claim that every short problem is hard.

PDF caches and rendered original pages remain outside the repository. Acquisition records retain URLs, exact byte hashes, page counts and read scopes. Cornell was read through the official web text; no PDF hash is invented for it. The mismatch is retained rather than hidden. All references and explanations in reference rows are English.

## Remaining limits

The core scopes support the declared chapter; full continuous and conditional expectation theory, covariance matrices, convergence theorem proofs and inference are scheduled later. The scanned examination archive was sampled at verified pages and is not claimed exhaustively classified. A bounded source pool, finite simulations and independent tests do not establish universal correctness or guarantee performance on every unseen examination question.
