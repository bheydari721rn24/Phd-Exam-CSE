Each replacement expands by row linearity into the original determinant plus $c$ times a determinant with two equal rows, which is zero. This proof uses no pivot division and does not assume invertibility. Induction over the finite sequence therefore preserves the determinant through every intermediate matrix. If the initial determinant is zero, it remains zero; if nonzero, every intermediate matrix stays invertible. Singular intermediates cannot appear in a sequence starting from a nonsingular matrix using only these invertible shears. Adding row scalings or swaps changes the ledger and must be accounted for separately.

**Transfer rule.** Prove an operation law without division when you need it to cover singular inputs as well.
@@ 78|A finite-field parity caveat|Original advanced boundary check
Over the field with two elements, evaluate the determinant of $\begin{bmatrix}1&1\\1&0\end{bmatrix}$ and explain why negative signs cannot be used to infer equal-row cancellation by dividing by two.
---SOLUTION---
The ordinary two-by-two formula gives $1\cdot0-1\cdot1=-1$, which equals one in this field, so the matrix is invertible. Minus one and plus one are identical, making row-swap sign reversal numerically invisible. An equation $D=-D$ therefore imposes no restriction on $D$; division by two is impossible because two equals zero. Equal-row determinants still vanish by the alternating determinant definition or direct paired-term cancellation. The conclusion remains true, but the common real-field proof that subtracts twice the determinant does not transfer unchanged.

**Transfer rule.** Check the field's characteristic before using parity signs or division by two in a proof.
@@ 79|Hadamard's volume bound with equality conditions|Original advanced geometry proof
For real column vectors $u_1,\ldots,u_n$, prove $|\det[u_1\cdots u_n]|\le\prod_i\|u_i\|$ and specify equality conditions.
---SOLUTION---
If the columns are dependent, the determinant is zero and the inequality follows immediately. Otherwise orthogonalize them in order without normalizing: each new residual subtracts combinations of earlier columns, preserving determinant, and its length is no larger than the original column length. The orthogonal-column parallelotope volume is the product of residual lengths, yielding the bound. For nonzero independent columns, equality occurs precisely when every residual already equals its original vector in length, meaning each column is orthogonal to its predecessors. If any column is zero, both sides vanish and equality holds trivially. Thus the zero-column boundary must be separated from the nonzero orthogonality characterization.

**Transfer rule.** Volume is bounded by the product of edge lengths, with nondegenerate equality exactly for mutually orthogonal edges.
@@ 80|A mixed examination workflow|Original hard synthesis
For $A(t)=\begin{bmatrix}t&1&1\\1&t&1\\1&1&t\end{bmatrix}$, find $\det A(t)$, $\det(\operatorname{adj}A(t))$, the singular ranks, and the unique-solution condition for $A(t)x=b$.
---SOLUTION---
The constant-off-diagonal decomposition gives determinant $(t-1)^2(t+2)$. For order three, the adjugate determinant is its square, namely $(t-1)^4(t+2)^2$, including the singular parameters. At $t=1$ the matrix is $J_3$ of rank one, so its adjugate is zero. At $t=-2$ rank is two, so its adjugate has rank one although its own determinant is zero. For every $b$ there is a unique solution exactly when $t\ne1,-2$. At excluded parameters, consistency depends on $b$ and cannot be answered from the determinant alone. This workflow keeps determinant, rank, adjugate rank, and system consistency as distinct conclusions.

**Transfer rule.** In mixed problems, derive the determinant first and then apply each downstream theorem with its own dimension and singular-case conditions.
