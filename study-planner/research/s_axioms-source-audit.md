# Source selection and boundary audit: s_axioms

Date: 2026-10-01. Subject: Probability and Statistics. Chapter: Sample Spaces, Events, and Probability Axioms. Only this chapter is active. Iranian entrance-exam archives remain deferred until the final month.

## Search boundary and comparison criteria

The survey covered accessible written introductory probability and measure-foundation material at MIT, Stanford, UC Berkeley, ETH Zurich, Oxford, Harvard, Cambridge, and Carnegie Mellon. The comparison prioritized (1) relevance to this chapter's modeling and axiom boundary, (2) explicit assumptions and mathematical correctness, (3) explanatory and worked-example value, (4) countable/infinite-space coverage, and (5) accessible readable text. This is a documented bounded survey, not a claim that every worldwide course was examined or that a global optimum was established. Listing a course or reading a search excerpt does not count as reviewing its text.

## Cross-source synthesis and mathematical decisions

1. Use a probability space (Omega, F, P), with countable additivity as the formal axiom; finite formulas follow as consequences.
2. Probability is assigned to events. A singleton can have mass zero, and not every subset of an uncountable space must be in F.
3. Equal probabilities require a modeling assumption. A coarse summary of equally likely detailed outcomes need not be uniformly distributed.
4. Derive inclusion-exclusion and feasibility through disjoint atomic regions; distinguish empty overlap from zero-mass overlap.
5. Derive continuity from below by disjoint increments and continuity from above by complements. Do not infer limit interchange for arbitrary event sequences.
6. Use union bounds without independence. The elementary tail-union proof is included as an advanced consequence; no converse requiring independence is asserted.
7. Course-derived reasoning types are independently worded with new parameters and full solutions. No source exercise statement or diagram is reproduced.

## Remaining scope limits

Full combinatorial counting and independence belong to s_counting; conditioning and Bayes belong to subsequent chapters. The note uses finite/countable event algebra, ordinary interval/area measures, and foundational limits, but does not construct the Lebesgue measure, prove a measure-extension theorem, or cover all graduate probability. Neither source selection nor a manuscript audit can guarantee perfect performance on all unseen questions.
