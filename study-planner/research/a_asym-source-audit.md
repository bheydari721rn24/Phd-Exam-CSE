## Synthesis and audit decisions

1. MIT, Stanford, and Princeton agree on eventual constants for O/Ω/Θ; CMU adds strict o/ω. The manuscript uses `f ∈ O(g)` and explains why `f = O(g)` is conventional shorthand rather than ordinary equality.
2. Stanford's constructive witnesses are extended to signed polynomial coefficients only after establishing eventual nonnegativity. Leading-term cancellation is performed before selecting a growth class.
3. CMU's oscillation case and Princeton's non-limit example motivate envelope methods. A nonexistent ratio limit does not imply incomparability.
4. Princeton's sum/product/transitivity laws are proved under eventually nonnegative functions. Inversion requires eventual positivity and a **two-sided** bound for Θ; a mere O bound does not give a reciprocal Θ bound.
5. The common hierarchy is justified for fixed exponents and bases; variable bases/exponents require direct logarithmic comparison. Factorial claims include elementary bounds rather than relying only on Stirling's approximation.
6. For multiple parameters, the admissible domain is explicit. The illustrative joint convention quantifies over both parameters sufficiently large; graphs may impose a separate relationship between vertices and edges. One-variable claims do not automatically transfer to unconstrained pairs.
7. All solved problems are original or independently worded adaptations of reasoning types in the four texts. No source exercise is reproduced verbatim; source-inspired types are labeled in the manuscript.
