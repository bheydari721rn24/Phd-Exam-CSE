## Mathematical review

The written sources and chapter proofs were compared for terminology, existence and uniqueness, empty carriers, image/codomain distinction, partiality, composition types, quantifier order, cancellation, inverse uniqueness, and choice assumptions. The text supplies full proofs for inverse existence, image/preimage laws and their equality conditions, quotient well-definedness, currying, inclusion–exclusion, the Stirling recurrence, Schröder–Bernstein, and Cantor's diagonal theorem. Original solutions address all relevant exercise families in the stated source reading boundary. Specific checks include the rational parity obstruction, interval limiting endpoints, closed-to-open interval bijection, natural-pair decoding, and monotone-sequence cardinality arguments.

Independent finite checking exhaustively enumerated 499 functions on domain and target sizes zero through four, 97,549 pairs of input subsets, 901 one-sided inverse candidates on smaller carriers, and 1,678 compatible composite pairs. It checked image/preimage laws, saturation, intersection/difference equality characterizations, inverse counts and empty-set exceptions, unrestricted/injective/onto/bijective/image-size counts, and example calculations. The natural-pair inverse was checked for 10,000 targets. These checks supplement the general written proofs; they do not establish correctness for arbitrary infinite sets by testing.

The JavaScript laboratory was independently compared against definition-based enumeration for 1,280 finite functions and 17,720 selected-subset cases. Its invalid inputs are rejected. Collision witnesses, missed targets, fibers, image, saturation, and inverse results matched the independent checks. The Python function-analysis routine printed in the lesson was executed from the manuscript and checked against independently constructed fibers and classifications.

The actual Cambridge PDF edition was identified from its title page rather than inferred from its URL.

The authored chapter was inspected in a local Edge browser at desktop width 1,280 pixels, mobile width 390 pixels, and A4-like print styling. All four figure captures were visually reviewed. SVG label geometry had no out-of-viewBox labels or overlapping text pairs. Graph crossings do not indicate extra vertices and are explained in captions. The mobile document stayed within its 390-pixel viewport, formula blocks had no detected overflow, and the diagrams fit their print containers. Small-screen figures retain readable labels inside local horizontal scroll regions.

The summation uses native MathML with semantic lower and upper limits and a stacked binomial coefficient. Inline mathematical indices use semantic subscript and superscript elements, including numeral indices and the positive-natural marker. SVG indices use explicitly styled tspans. Unconverted carets and mathematical underscores were eliminated. Links have no underlining. A detected mobile overflow in a long inline calculation was corrected by allowing mathematical runs to wrap on small screens. Laboratory presets, dropdown editing, and subset toggling were exercised in the browser.
