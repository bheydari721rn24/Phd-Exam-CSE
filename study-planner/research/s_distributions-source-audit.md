# Discrete distributions: evaluated written-course pool

## Search boundary and selection

Nine university candidates were compared using course inventories and accessible chapter text. Five courses were actually read. Oxford, Cambridge, Berkeley and MIT form the core; Stanford supplies an application extension. Selection follows chapter-level scientific coverage, derivation quality, support and parameter clarity, computational interpretation and accessible written evidence. An inventory, title or download is not counted as a lecture reading. This is a bounded documented search; it is not possible to certify that every course worldwide has been evaluated or that four courses are globally best.

## Synthesis decisions

Oxford supplies the algebraic framework; Berkeley derives infinite waiting laws; MIT reconciles fixed horizons and counting conventions; Cambridge fills the negative-binomial and hypergeometric catalogue; Stanford supplies engineering applications and examples of model assumptions failing. Full stopped-sequence, subset-count and finite-population waiting proofs develop the displayed formulas. These derivations are verified independently by exhaustive finite enumeration and symbolic identities.

The source discrepancy register is part of the lesson: positive and zero-based geometric conventions are both stated; two Poisson modes occur when the positive parameter is an integer; rate must be multiplied by exposure; finite binomial counts remain distinct from unbounded Poisson limits; a shared random probability changes the law and variance; and independence is never inferred merely from identical marginal probabilities.
