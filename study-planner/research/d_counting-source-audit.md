# Counting chapter: source selection and reading audit

Review date: October 5, 2026. Scope: the sole new d_counting draft. The comparison below covers eight located, accessible course candidates or course-linked resources. It does not claim that all courses at all universities have been enumerated, ranked, or read. Screened material is explicitly distinguished from scoped reading. The four selected primary courses are from four different universities and were chosen for complementary chapter coverage, proof depth, instructional clarity, and concrete exercise patterns.

## Synthesis and source-specific corrections

The lesson synchronizes proof obligations rather than concatenating notes. Sections on finite models and fibers align MIT 14.1–14.5, Berkeley Note 12, Stanford Notes 1–2, and CMU Lecture 7. Fixed-content words align MIT 14.6, Stanford Note 2 Sections 1 and 4, and CMU Lecture 8. Allocations align MIT 14.1/14.5, Berkeley Note 12's multiset encoding, Stanford Note 2 Section 5, and CMU Lecture 8 pages 3–4. Identities align MIT 14.10, Berkeley Note 12.5's combinatorial proofs, and CMU Lecture 8 pages 10–13. Stanford's tree example supplies a second nonuniform-fiber model. Gaps, periodic rotation orbits, mixed bounds, and the reflection application receive independent proofs because no claim is made that every selected course develops all those extensions.

Stanford Note 2's lower-bound allocation discussion reuses the old x-variable names after changing the total. The new lesson uses y_i=x_i-l_i and states its inverse, avoiding a changed meaning for the old variables. A duplicated edition label in the constrained-book prose is not reproduced. CMU Lecture 8's positive-allocation shift is expressed unambiguously as y_i=x_i-1. Berkeley's two-endpoint bit-string example needs length at least two before the two fixed positions are distinct; the chapter's corresponding applications state feasibility rather than applying the expression to length one. CMU Lecture 7's straight-hand slide uses nine rank runs; conventional ace-low-inclusive poker uses ten. This chapter does not adopt that convention-sensitive straight count. Its independent two-pair example states a six-rank, four-suit deck explicitly.

Original public materials were read for the stated scope. The local downloader timed out on the current MIT URL, but an existing original 2015 textbook PDF/text cache was read. Berkeley and Stanford original PDFs were downloaded and text-extracted. CMU originals were accessed through the web PDF reader after native requests failed. Access failures are retained in research/d_counting-source-downloads.json. No protected full course or entire question bank is republished.

## Coverage boundary

Covered: finite models; sum, product, bijection, and fiber rules; permutations, roles, subsets, multisets, fixed stock; blocks, gaps, cyclic arrangements, periodicity; labeled allocations and nonempty partitions; lower/upper/weighted constraints; binomial and multinomial choices; identities and parity; monotone indices, paths, reflection, and exact evaluation. Deferred systematically: full inclusion–exclusion, derangements and general surjections via that principle, pigeonhole applications, recurrence solving, and generating-function techniques. The published quality audit maps the model checks and remaining limitations.
