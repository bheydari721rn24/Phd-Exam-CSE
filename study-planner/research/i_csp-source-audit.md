# Constraint satisfaction: written-source selection and reconciliation

Date: 18 October 2026. This is a bounded, chapter-specific source review. The chapter remains an author-review draft until its mathematical and browser gates pass; student approval is a separate status.

## Discovery boundary and selection

Seven offerings at six universities were evaluated for accessible written instruction on finite CSP modeling, search, propagation and structure. Stanford CS221, Berkeley CS188, CMU 15-281, MIT 16.410, MIT 6.034, Cornell CS4700 and Harvard CS50 AI form the documented candidate pool. This is not every course worldwide, and no globally best-course claim is made. MIT 6.034 was identified but its CSP instructional body was not read in this pass; it is not counted toward the university minimum or used to support a technical claim.

The four complementary primary offerings are Stanford CS221, Berkeley CS188, CMU 15-281 and MIT 16.410. Selection used actual written scope, explicit mathematical/algorithmic detail, complementary coverage and access, rather than university name alone. Stanford contributes factor modeling, value/variable ordering, weighted examples and approximate search. Berkeley supplies the clearest directly accessible tree/cutset treatment. CMU provides an independent integrated algorithm/heuristic account. MIT contributes precise constraint lectures, explicit worklist/forward-checking comparisons and conflict-directed reasoning. Cornell and Harvard are additional substantive comparisons, not substitutes for missing primary reading: Cornell strengthens consistency quantifiers and extensional-table reasoning; Harvard provides a second implementation/crossword perspective.

## Acquisition and actual reading are distinct

Official Stanford module files and Harvard notes were freshly acquired and their byte fingerprints recorded. Berkeley and CMU bodies were read through the web reader despite failed direct local acquisition. MIT/Cornell PDF text was read through the web reader; direct local PDF downloads timed out or failed. No local byte fingerprint or visual-page claim is invented for those university PDFs. The research ledger retains these limitations rather than replacing acquisition failures with a fictitious download success.

The included peer reading report records exact PDF page scopes, independent mathematical reconciliations and inaccessible source content. The original source PDFs are not redistributed. The chapter's own algorithm is fully printed and tested; it is an independently written finite binary implementation, not a claim that every source pseudocode listing is executable as printed.

## Corrections and conventions resolved before synthesis

1. **Domain notation:** a domain is set-valued. A Stanford display using scalar membership for a domain is interpreted through its prose and corrected to the explicit finite set.
2. **Actual neighbors:** Stanford's NT forward-checking prose mentions NSW, which is not adjacent to NT in the displayed standard map. Every chapter player instead receives its own declared exact edges; source prose is not used to invent a nonedge.
3. **LCV scores:** counting deleted neighbor values differs from counting affected neighboring variables. The distinction appears in CMU/Harvard prose and is resolved with an exact opposite-ranking counterexample, IC12.
4. **Forward-checking cost:** a singleton head costs one predicate test per candidate neighbor value. The chapter distinguishes $O(\delta d)$ from generic arbitrary-head $O(\delta d^2)$ revision.
5. **Algorithm contracts:** CMU wrapper naming and incomplete restoration/initial-empty-domain details are repaired in the chapter's independent implementation. MIT extracted FC indentation is not treated as reliable executable control flow. A possible MIT Lecture6 queue-orientation typo remains a text-extraction suspicion, not a visually verified typesetting error.
6. **Queue membership:** enqueue existing incoming neighbors only; conjoin predicates on a pair; store reverse relations as transposes; omit the immediate reverse only under that contract.
7. **Complexity notation:** e counts directed arcs in the chapter's exact statements. Dense $O(n^2d^3)$ and sparse $O(ed^3)$ are reconciled, and lookup/input/output costs are stated separately.
8. **Cornell arithmetic:** n variables of domain size d have $d^n$ tuples, not $n^d$. Also $8^8=16777216$. Historical queen-completion complexity claims and unsupported unconditional exponential lower bounds are omitted.
9. **Consistency quantifiers:** every consistent assignment to k−1 variables must extend to a selected kth variable. This is stronger than merely finding some solution on every k-variable subset. Strong consistency includes all lower levels; an empty unary base is not a construction certificate.
10. **Tree/cutset bounds:** the tree premise, empty-domain checks, boundary/internal constraints, enumeration and given-versus-found cutsets are explicit. The residual-only term cannot be the complete cost when c=n.
11. **Weighted factors:** nonnegative products generalize hard constraints; they are not automatically probabilities. A partial product is not an upper bound when remaining factors can exceed one.
12. **Approximate/local search:** beam selection complexity includes its scoring/selection assumptions. Finite local state spaces do not prevent equal-score cycles, and a zero unaffected factor invalidates an unqualified strict-global-improvement claim.

## Course-prompt inventory and originality

The full chapter bank contains fifty original or attributed instructional problems. Source-derived prompts include MIT's four-queen nonadjacent-column table (IC02), three-variable propagation (IC06), AC1/AC3 comparison (IC38), conflict-set merge (IC36), Cornell's extensional relation (IC05), and Stanford's sum-support, weighted voting, scheduling, sculpture and verification examples (IC03,IC33,IC45–IC47). Numerical variations, proof requests and solutions are explicitly authored and independently checked. IC07 compares the common odd-cycle counterexample across courses.

This is a scoped prompt inventory, not a reproduction of every assignment question from every candidate offering. Hidden, protected or unread exercises are not silently labeled covered. The remaining problems develop counts, proofs, counterexamples, costs and boundary cases from the synthesized concepts. Authorship labels distinguish adaptation from independent examination-style construction.

Four original Iranian master's items were read from full original-page images and pinned archive metadata. No official key was verified; their answers are independently derived. No usable doctoral CSP item was verified in the bounded archive pass, so no doctoral provenance is fabricated. Two ambiguities are prominently preserved: graph edge versus directed arc in1402Q74, and the unstated tree premise in1405Q74. The1404 map's three-color visualization is explicitly a teaching assumption, not an extra printed premise.

Finite exact checks and browser checks support the delivered chapter; they do not prove literal universal correctness, exhaustive worldwide coverage or perfect performance on unseen questions.
