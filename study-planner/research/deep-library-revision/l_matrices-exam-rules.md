### 13.8 Detailed examination decisions: sixteen construction rules

These rules supplement the fifty complete rules above. Each decision includes a formula, its domain, a checkable example and a specific failure mode. Read the full proofs and answer derivations before using this section for retrieval.

**51. Count symmetric parameters before applying additional equations.** The symmetric $n\times n$ space has dimension $n(n+1)/2$, because each reflected off-diagonal pair has one parameter. Trace zero subtracts one independent functional for positive $n$. In size five, an additional $a_{12}=0$ condition gives $15-2=13$. Repeating $a_{21}=0$ after symmetry does not subtract again. Prove independence of constraints with explicit witness matrices when they can overlap.

**52. Separate lattice cardinality from real rank.** The real system $x_1+x_2+x_3=5$, $x_1+\cdots+x_5=20$ has affine dimension three. Its nonnegative integer solutions number $\binom72\cdot16=336$, after subtraction exposes disjoint groups. Adding $21+16=37$ chooses either group, whereas a complete tuple requires both. The original source's additive alternative is 37; a previous transcription as 32 was wrong.

**53. A lower induced-norm bound does not assert a positive quadratic form.** From $\|Ax\|\ge\theta\|x\|$, infer trivial kernel, square-map invertibility and $\|A^{-1}\|\le1/\theta$. The witness $A=-I$, $\theta=1$ satisfies the norm inequality while $x^TAx<0$ for nonzero real $x$. Magnitude control and sign control are different mathematical claims. If nonreal eigenvalues are considered with an arbitrary real norm, justify the complex extension rather than silently substituting a complex eigenvector into a real-only hypothesis.

**54. Preserve an authentic question's hypothesis before using a memorized formula.** A merely orthogonal generator $u=(2,0)$ gives $\sum\langle x,u\rangle u=\operatorname{diag}(4,0)x$, which is not idempotent. True projection divides by $\langle u,u\rangle$, or uses a unit basis. In the original Q31, both idempotence and the reversed norm inequality can fail literally; there is no asserted unique literal key. Under the added orthonormal interpretation, contraction follows from Pythagoras, and the full-space case gives equality.

**55. Identity shifts change an eigenvalue on the all-equal line.** For $J=qq^T$ with $q=(1,\ldots,1)$, $Jq=nq$ and $Jz=0$ whenever the coordinate sum of $z$ is zero. Hence $\det(I+J)=n+1$, not $2^n$ from its original displayed diagonal. At size five the determinant is six. A singular rank-one addend does not force the identity-plus-addend to be singular. The inverse $I-J/(n+1)$ gives an independent product check.

**56. Triangular structure guarantees a diagonal-product determinant; it is not necessary for an isolated numerical coincidence.** The nontriangular matrix $\begin{pmatrix}1&1&1\\0&1&1\\1&0&2\end{pmatrix}$ has determinant two and diagonal product two. Thus the word “only” would make the original authored scope alternative false. For upper triangular matrices, the permutation formula proves the guarantee because only the identity permutation can survive. An example can refute necessity without replacing the sufficient structural theorem.

**57. Inverse shear negates the coupling rather than reciprocating it.** For $S=\begin{pmatrix}1&t\\0&1\end{pmatrix}$, the inverse has upper-right $-t$. The product's upper-right is $t+(-t)=0$. At $t=5$, proposing $1/5$ leaves $26/5$ in that product entry. At $t=0$, both maps are identity even though an off-diagonal reciprocal is undefined. Check the inverse on both sides and keep zero-entry behavior separate from full-map invertibility.

**58. Weighted permutation trace counts fixed coordinates.** If $D=\operatorname{diag}(d_1,\ldots,d_n)$ and $P$ is a permutation matrix, then $\operatorname{tr}(DP)=\sum_{i:P_{ii}=1}d_i$. A no-fixed-coordinate cycle in size five gives zero; identity gives $\sum_i d_i$. A nontrivial cycle requires $n\ge2$. The rule is an entry calculation and does not use false trace multiplicativity.

**59. Determinant scaling gains one scalar factor per row.** The product $(2I_n)(3I_n)=6I_n$ has determinant $6^n$ and trace $6n$. At size five these are 7776 and 30. Determinant multiplicativity needs compatible square factors but does not require their commutation. Determinants are not additive: $\det(2I_2)=4$ while $\det I_2+\det I_2=2$.

**60. A two-step nilpotent contribution selects distinct chronological slots.** For $N_{12}=N_{23}=1$, $N^3=0$ and $(I+N)^k_{13}=\binom{k}{2}$. The exact values at powers five, seven, eight and ten are 10, 21, 28 and 45. Squaring $k$ allows impossible equal-slot actions; using $\binom{k+1}{2}$ gives an off-by-one sum. The recurrence $t_{k+1}=t_k+k$, $t_0=0$ verifies both the formula and the simulation's counters.

**61. Full rectangular factor rank must be interpreted through the intermediate space.** If $U:n\times2$ is injective and $V:2\times n$ is onto, then $\operatorname{rank}(UV)=2$ and $\ker(UV)=\ker V$. At $n=2$ this product is invertible; at larger sizes it is singular. For $n\ge4$, an embedding into the first two coordinates composed with a reader of the third and fourth has rank two and square zero, while the reverse product is zero. Never infer reverse-product rank or an inverse merely from the two factor ranks.

**62. A new matrix column is a new-basis coordinate list of an actual image.** For basis vectors $(1,1),(1,-1)$, convert $(x,y)$ to $((x+y)/2,(x-y)/2)$. Applying $T=\operatorname{diag}(2,5)$ to the second basis vector gives $(2,-5)$, so the first coefficient is $-3/2$. Copying that image into the new column omits the output conversion. Similarity uses the same ordered basis on both sides; independent input/output bases give $C^{-1}TB$ instead.

**63. A triangular block inverse does not require an inverse of the coupling.** The upper-right inverse of $\begin{pmatrix}A&C\\0&D\end{pmatrix}$ is $-A^{-1}CD^{-1}$ when the two diagonal blocks are invertible. If both are identity it is $-C$; if the lower block is $2I$ it is $-C/2$. A rectangular $C$ may have no ordinary inverse at all. Derive the formula by first recovering the lower coordinates, then subtracting their contribution from the upper coordinates.

**64. A Gram kernel proof is a positivity proof.** For real matrices, $A^TAx=0$ implies $0=x^TA^TAx=\|Ax\|_2^2$, forcing $Ax=0$. This proves $\ker(A^TA)=\ker A$ and equal ranks, not rank equal to every row dimension or unconditional invertibility. For the complex column $(1,i)$, ordinary $A^TA$ is zero despite injectivity, so use $A^*A$. A semidefinite weight requires a separate test for killed output directions.

**65. Rectangular trace equality discards shape information.** For $A=[I_2\ 0]$ and $B=A^T$, $AB=I_2$ while $BA=\operatorname{diag}(1,1,0)$. Both traces are two; their determinants are one and zero. The trace identity comes from regrouping the same scalar products, not from matrix equality. Different shapes cannot be equated as arrays, even when a scalar invariant agrees.

**66. Strict positivity excludes null endpoint subspaces.** In dimension $d\ge2$, the equicorrelation matrix has coefficients $1+(d-1)a$ on its all-equal line and $1-a$ on its sum-zero complement. Both are positive exactly for $-1/(d-1)<a<1$. In size four this gives $-1/3<a<1$. At the lower endpoint the all-equal vector is null; at the upper endpoint every sum-zero vector is null. Positive determinant or positive entries alone cannot replace this full-space quadratic argument.
