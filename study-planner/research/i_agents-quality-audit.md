## Mathematical review

The finite-action memoryless-policy intersection criterion is stated with its quantifiers and proved in both directions. Bayesian prediction and conditioning are separated; impossible observations never receive invented posteriors. Information-value inequalities require the stated fixed action set and passive sensor assumptions. Vacuum-world counts distinguish physical states from belief supports. Abstractions preserve goal status, legal actions, transition images and costs rather than merely merging similar-looking pictures.

The automated evidence reports 7,470 exact checks. The editable two-state, two-action information laboratory was compared on 601 independently enumerated inputs against a Python Fraction oracle that evaluates all four contingent policies. Tests cover impossible branches, ties, negative utilities, uninformative and perfect sensors, large fractions and invalid inputs. This is evidence for the declared finite models; it is not a proof of all possible implementations or unseen exam performance.
